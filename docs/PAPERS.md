## 1. Neocortex & Laminar Microcircuits

### Douglas & Martin (1991, 2004) — Canonical Microcircuit of the Neocortex
- **Citations**: 
  - Douglas, R. J., & Martin, K. A. (1991). *A functional microcircuit for cat visual cortex*. The Journal of Physiology, 440(1), 735-769.
  - Douglas, R. J., & Martin, K. A. (2004). *Neuronal circuits of the neocortex*. Annual Review of Neuroscience, 27, 419-451.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  The neocortex preserves a conserved 6-layer cytoarchitectonic computational unit across all sensory and associative areas:
  - **Layer IV**: Stellate interneurons receive primary specific thalamocortical afferents.
  - **Layers II/III**: Superficial pyramidal cells perform intracortical and corticocortical associative integration.
  - **Layer V**: Large pyramidal neurons project subcortical and motor commands to the basal ganglia, brainstem, and spinal cord.
  - **Layer VI**: Multiform neurons generate modulatory corticothalamic feedback loops to thalamic relay nuclei.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/telencephalon/neocortex/layer.py`](../bib2/telencephalon/neocortex/layer.py) (`CanonicalMicrocircuit`), [`bib2/telencephalon/neocortex/areas.py`](../bib2/telencephalon/neocortex/areas.py) (`CorticalAreaRegistry`).

## 2. Sleep Architecture, SWR Memory Replay, and Synaptic Homeostasis

### Tononi & Cirelli (2003, 2014) — Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis (SHY)
- **Citations**:
  - Tononi, G., & Cirelli, C. (2003). *Sleep and synaptic homeostasis: a hypothesis*. Brain Research Bulletin, 62(2), 143-150.
  - Tononi, G., & Cirelli, C. (2014). *Sleep and the price of plasticity: from synaptic and cellular homeostasis to memory consolidation and integration*. Neuron, 81(1), 12-34.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  Wakefulness produces net synaptic potentiation across cortical networks, which increases energy consumption, occupies cellular space, and saturates learning capacity. Slow-Wave Sleep (SWS) mediates a global, proportional downscaling of synaptic weights, renormalizing synaptic strength while preserving signal-to-noise contrast and preventing catastrophic forgetting.
- **Mathematical Form**:
  $$W_{\text{post}} = W_{\text{pre}} \cdot \gamma, \quad \gamma \in (0, 1)$$
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/sleep/shy.py`](../bib2/sleep/shy.py) (`TononiSHYDownscaling`), verified to produce a 42.7% synaptic reduction during Slow-Wave Sleep in [`bib2/benchmark.py`](../bib2/benchmark.py).

### Buzsáki (2015) — Hippocampal Sharp-Wave Ripples (SWRs)
- **Citation**:
  - Buzsáki, G. (2015). *Hippocampal sharp wave-ripple: A cognitive biomarker for episodic memory and planning*. Hippocampus, 25(10), 1073-1188.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  During non-REM Slow-Wave Sleep and behavioral immobility, hippocampal CA3/CA1 pyramidal networks spontaneously fire high-frequency (150–250 Hz) transient oscillatory events termed Sharp-Wave Ripples. SWRs compress and replay daytime episodic trajectory sequences at 10x–20x real-world speed, transferring labile hippocampal memories into permanent neocortical association networks.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/sleep/ripples.py`](../bib2/sleep/ripples.py) (`SharpWaveRippleEngine`).

### Borbély (1982) — Two-Process Model of Sleep Regulation
- **Citation**:
  - Borbély, A. A. (1982). *A two-process model of sleep regulation*. Human Neurobiology, 1(3), 195-204.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  Sleep timing and duration emerge from the nonlinear interaction between two independent drives:
  - **Process S (Homeostatic)**: Sleep pressure accumulates monotonically during wakefulness as adenosine builds up from metabolic activity, decaying exponentially during sleep.
  - **Process C (Circadian)**: Oscillating sinusoidal alertness generated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) aligned to the environmental day/night light cycle.
  - Sleep gates open when $S$ exceeds $C$.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/sleep/orchestrator.py`](../bib2/sleep/orchestrator.py) (`SleepOrchestrator`).

### Nedergaard and colleagues (2012, 2013) — Glymphatic Fluid Transport & AQP4 Waste Wash
- **Citations**:
  - Iliff, J. J., Wang, M., Liao, Y., Plogg, B. A., Peng, W., Gundersen, G. A., ... & Nedergaard, M. (2012). *A paravascular pathway facilitates CSF flow through the brain parenchyma and the clearance of interstitial solutes, including amyloid $\beta$*. Science Translational Medicine, 4(147), 147ra111.
  - Xie, L., Kang, H., Xu, Q., Chen, M. J., Liao, Y., Thiyagarajan, M., ... & Nedergaard, M. (2013). *Sleep drives metabolite clearance from the adult brain*. Science, 342(6156), 373-377.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  During Slow-Wave Sleep, the brain's interstitial space expands by ~60%, driven by astrocytic aquaporin-4 ($AQP4$) water channels. This facilitates convective bulk flow of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) along periarterial pathways into the parenchymal extracellular matrix, washing away accumulated metabolic solutes (such as adenosine and beta-amyloid) into the perivenous and deep cervical lymphatic systems.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/sleep/glymphatic.py`](../bib2/sleep/glymphatic.py) (`GlymphaticEngine`).

## 3. Basal Ganglia Action Selection & Striatal Plasticity

### Mink (1996) & Frank (2004) — Basal Ganglia Tripartite Gating Circuits
- **Citations**:
  - Mink, J. W. (1996). *The basal ganglia: focused selection and inhibition of competing motor programs*. Progress in Neurobiology, 50(4), 381-425.
  - Frank, M. J. (2004). *By carrot or by stick: cognitive reinforcement learning in parkinsonism*. Science, 306(5703), 1940-1943.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  The basal ganglia execute action selection through three interacting loops:
  1. **Direct Pathway (D1 MSNs)**: Provides focused GABAergic inhibition to GPi/SNr output nuclei, disinhibiting the thalamus and releasing the selected action (*Go*).
  2. **Indirect Pathway (D2 MSNs)**: Projects via GPe and STN to excite GPi/SNr output nuclei, suppressing competing actions (*NoGo*).
  3. **Hyperdirect Pathway (STN)**: Monosynaptic pyramidal drive to STN triggers immediate, global inhibition of thalamocortical channels (*Emergency Brake*).
  4. **Dopaminergic Gain**: Nigrostriatal dopamine excites D1 MSNs while inhibiting D2 MSNs. Positive reward-prediction errors (RPE) strengthen D1 synapses, while negative RPE strengthens D2 synapses.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/telencephalon/basal_ganglia.py`](../bib2/telencephalon/basal_ganglia.py) (`BasalGangliaComplex`).

## 4. Limbic System & Hippocampal Trisynaptic Circuit

### Papez (1937) — The Circuit of Papez
- **Citation**:
  - Papez, J. W. (1937). *A proposed mechanism of emotion*. Archives of Neurology & Psychiatry, 38(4), 725-743.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  Anatomical loop essential for episodic memory consolidation:
  $$\text{Hippocampus} \xrightarrow{\text{Fornix}} \text{Mammillary Bodies} \xrightarrow{\text{Vicq d'Azyr}} \text{Anterior Thalamus} \xrightarrow{\text{Internal Capsule}} \text{Cingulate Gyrus} \xrightarrow{\text{Cingulum}} \text{Entorhinal Cortex} \to \text{Hippocampus}$$
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/telencephalon/limbic.py`](../bib2/telencephalon/limbic.py) (`CircuitOfPapez`).

### O'Reilly & McClelland (1994) — Hippocampal Pattern Separation & Auto-Association
- **Citation**:
  - O'Reilly, R. C., & McClelland, J. L. (1994). *Hippocampal conjunctive encoding, storage, and recall: Avoiding a trade-off*. Hippocampus, 4(6), 661-682.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  - **Dentate Gyrus (DG)**: Extreme expansion and k-winners-take-all (KWTA) sparse coding separates overlapping sensory representations to eliminate catastrophic interference.
  - **CA3**: Dense recurrent axon collaterals form an auto-associative attractor network capable of complete pattern recall from partial or corrupted sensory cues:
    $$\Delta W_{ij} = \frac{\eta}{\|p\|^2} p_i p_j$$
  - **CA1**: Converts spatial/associative CA3 attractor codes into temporal sequence outputs sent to the subiculum.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/telencephalon/limbic.py`](../bib2/telencephalon/limbic.py) (`DentateGyrus`, `CA3AutoAssociative`, `CA1Sequencer`, `HippocampalFormation`).

## 5. Cerebellum & Sensorimotor Forward Model

### Albus (1971), Ito (1982), & Wolpert (1998) — Cerebellar Forward Predictor
- **Citations**:
  - Albus, J. S. (1971). *A theory of cerebellar function*. Mathematical Biosciences, 10(1-2), 25-61.
  - Ito, M. (1982). *Cerebellar control of the vestibulo-ocular reflex: around the flocculus hypothesis*. Annual Review of Neuroscience, 5(1), 275-296.
  - Wolpert, D. M., Miall, R. C., & Kawato, M. (1998). *Internal models in the cerebellum*. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2(9), 338-347.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  - **Granule Layer**: Expands sensory and motor efference inputs into a high-dimensional parallel fiber space, firing simple spikes at 10–50 Hz.
  - **Climbing Fibers**: Originating exclusively from the Inferior Olivary nucleus, climbing fibers fire complex spikes at 1–2 Hz carrying sensorimotor prediction error signals.
  - **Long-Term Depression (LTD)**: Coincident parallel fiber and climbing fiber activation stimulates Purkinje cell $Ca^{2+}/\text{PKC}$ cascades, internalizing AMPA receptors and reducing Purkinje inhibition on the Deep Cerebellar Nuclei (DCN).
  - **Smith Forward Predictor**: Predicts the sensory consequences of motor commands before delayed peripheral feedback arrives, applying real-time micro-corrections.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/cerebellum/cortex.py`](../bib2/cerebellum/cortex.py) (`CerebellarCortex`), [`bib2/cerebellum/nuclei.py`](../bib2/cerebellum/nuclei.py) (`DeepCerebellarNuclei`), [`bib2/cerebellum/forward_model.py`](../bib2/cerebellum/forward_model.py) (`CerebellumEngine`).

## 6. Brainstem Pacemakers & Vegetative Stability

### Smith et al. (1991) — Pre-Bötzinger Complex Rhythmogenesis
- **Citation**:
  - Smith, J. C., Ellenberger, H. H., Ballanyi, K., Richter, D. W., & Feldman, J. L. (1991). *Pre-Bötzinger complex: a brainstem region that may generate respiratory rhythm in mammals*. Science, 254(5032), 726-729.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  The pre-Bötzinger complex in the ventrolateral medulla generates autonomous eupneic respiratory rhythm through synchronized $Dbx1^+ / Vglut2^+$ glutamatergic pacemaker interneurons. Bursting is driven by persistent sodium ($I_{\text{NaP}}$) and calcium-activated non-selective cation ($I_{\text{CAN}}$) currents, and is selectively inhibited by $\mu$-opioid receptor agonists.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/brainstem/medulla.py`](../bib2/brainstem/medulla.py) (`PreBotzingerComplex`).

### Dampney (1994) — Medullary Baroreflex Circuit
- **Citation**:
  - Dampney, R. A. (1994). *Functional organization of central pathways regulating the cardiovascular system*. Physiological Reviews, 74(2), 323-364.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  Negative feedback loop maintaining systemic arterial blood pressure:
  $$\text{Baroreceptors (CN IX/X)} \to \text{NTS} \xrightarrow{\text{Glu}} \text{CVLM} \xrightarrow{\text{GABA}} \text{RVLM} \dashv \text{Spinal Sympathetic Outflow}$$
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/brainstem/medulla.py`](../bib2/brainstem/medulla.py) (`MedullaryBaroreflex`).

## 7. Spinal Cord Cytoarchitecture & Reflexes

### Rexed (1952, 1954) — Cytoarchitectonic Atlas of the Spinal Cord
- **Citations**:
  - Rexed, B. (1952). *The cytoarchitectonic organization of the spinal cord in the cat*. Journal of Comparative Neurology, 96(3), 415-495.
  - Rexed, B. (1954). *A cytoarchitectonic atlas of the spinal cord in the cat*. Journal of Comparative Neurology, 100(2), 297-379.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  Spinal gray matter is organized into 10 laminar zones:
  - **Laminae I–IV**: Dorsal horn receiving nociceptive, thermal, and light cutaneous touch afferents.
  - **Laminae V–VI**: Mechanical nociceptive convergence and proprioceptive relay.
  - **Lamina VII**: Intermediate zone containing Clarke’s column and autonomic preganglionic neurons.
  - **Lamina VIII**: Commissural interneurons driving crossed-extensor motor coordination.
  - **Lamina IX**: Alpha and gamma motor neuron somatic effector columns.
  - **Lamina X**: Central canal periependymal gray matter.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/spinal/cord.py`](../bib2/spinal/cord.py) (`SpinalCordEngine`), [`bib2/spinal/reflexes.py`](../bib2/spinal/reflexes.py) (`StretchReflexCircuit`, `CrossedExtensorCircuit`), [`bib2/spinal/tracts.py`](../bib2/spinal/tracts.py) (`AscendingTractsConduction`, `DescendingCorticospinalTract`).

## 8. Neurochemistry, HPA Stress Axis, and Molecular Plasticity

### Goldman, Hodgkin, & Katz (1943, 1949) — Steady-State Membrane Potential
- **Citations**:
  - Goldman, D. E. (1943). *Potential, impedance, and rectification in membranes*. Journal of General Physiology, 27(1), 37-60.
  - Hodgkin, A. L., & Katz, B. (1949). *The effect of sodium ions on the electrical activity of the giant axon of the squid*. The Journal of Physiology, 108(1), 37-77.
- **Mathematical Form**:
  $$V_m = \frac{RT}{F} \ln \left( \frac{P_{\text{K}}[\text{K}^+]_{\text{out}} + P_{\text{Na}}[\text{Na}^+]_{\text{out}} + P_{\text{Cl}}[\text{Cl}^-]_{\text{in}}}{P_{\text{K}}[\text{K}^+]_{\text{in}} + P_{\text{Na}}[\text{Na}^+]_{\text{in}} + P_{\text{Cl}}[\text{Cl}^-]_{\text{out}}} \right)$$
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/chemistry/plasticity.py`](../bib2/chemistry/plasticity.py) (`GoldmanHodgkinKatzPotential`).

### Malenka & Bear (2004) — LTP and LTD Molecular Plasticity
- **Citation**:
  - Malenka, R. C., & Bear, M. F. (2004). *LTP and LTD: an embarrassment of riches*. Neuron, 44(1), 5-21.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  - **Early LTP (E-LTP)**: Strong NMDA receptor activation drives rapid $Ca^{2+}$ influx, activating CaMKII to phosphorylate and insert AMPA receptors into the postsynaptic density.
  - **Late LTP (L-LTP)**: Persistent signaling activates CREB-dependent protein translation to structurally enlarge dendritic spines.
  - **LTD**: Low, prolonged $Ca^{2+}$ levels recruit protein phosphatase calcineurin, driving clathrin-dependent AMPA receptor endocytosis.
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/chemistry/plasticity.py`](../bib2/chemistry/plasticity.py) (`PlasticityEngine`).

### Ulrich-Lai & Herman (2009) — Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Stress Axis
- **Citation**:
  - Ulrich-Lai, Y. M., & Herman, J. P. (2009). *Neural regulation of endocrine and autonomic stress responses*. Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 10(6), 397-409.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  Endocrine stress cascade:
  $$\text{PVN Hypothalamus (CRH)} \to \text{Anterior Pituitary (ACTH)} \to \text{Adrenal Cortex (Cortisol)} \dashv \text{Negative Feedback at PVN \& Pituitary}$$
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/chemistry/hpa.py`](../bib2/chemistry/hpa.py) (`HPAAxis`).

## 9. Language Neurobiology & Arcuate Fasciculus

### Wernicke (1874), Broca (1861), & Geschwind (1970) — Dual-Stream Wernicke-Broca Architecture
- **Citations**:
  - Broca, P. (1861). *Remarques sur le siège de la faculté du langage articulé*. Bulletin de la Société Anatomique, 36, 330-357.
  - Wernicke, C. (1874). *Der aphasische Symptomencomplex: eine psychologische Studie auf anatomischer Basis*. Cohn.
  - Geschwind, N. (1970). *The organization of language and the brain*. Science, 170(3961), 940-944.
  - Hickok, G., & Poeppel, D. (2007). *The cortical organization of speech processing*. Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 8(5), 393-402.
- **Neurobiological Principle**:
  Language processing bifurcates into dorsal (articulatory/phonetic motor loop) and ventral (semantic lexical comprehension) pathways:
  $$\text{Primary Auditory (A1 / BA 41/42)} \to \text{Wernicke (BA 22)} \xrightarrow{\text{Arcuate Fasciculus}} \text{Broca (BA 44/45)} \to \text{CN XII Motor Speech}$$
- **Code Mapping**: [`bib2/telencephalon/neocortex/areas.py`](../bib2/telencephalon/neocortex/areas.py) (`process_language_repetition`), [`bib2/adapters/language.py`](../bib2/adapters/language.py) (`LanguageAdapter`).
